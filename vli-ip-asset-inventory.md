# Velocity Lab Innovation: Product-Specific IP Asset Inventory

**Purpose:** Working inventory for patent counsel, product leadership, and prospective FAI/CIAM discussions. It identifies assets inferred from the current public product description. Each item requires a factual owner, creator, date, and evidence record before it can be relied upon for filing or enforcement.

## 1. Technical invention candidates — screen for patentability

These are not patents merely because they are ideas. They are candidates only if Velocity Lab has a **new, non-obvious, technically implemented solution** that can be described in enough detail to reproduce and test.

| Candidate technical asset | What VLI should define precisely | Initial protection decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Calibrated multi-camera competition-volume set-up** | Camera placement, calibration routine, fiducial/anchor method, automatic health checks, re-calibration trigger, and the resulting technical improvement in tracking availability or accuracy. | Consider patent review only if the method produces a measurable technical result beyond normal OptiTrack configuration. Otherwise retain the deployment recipe as a trade secret. |
| **Drone-ball / striker identity method** | How the system combines position, LED/team/striker status, and match state to identify the legally relevant drone under occlusion or collisions. | Strongest patent candidate if VLI has a new sensor-fusion or identity-confidence method that is robust in a defined live-match environment. |
| **Spatial-event decision engine** | Formal event definitions for goal-ring crossing, line/boundary events, return-to-side obligations, penalties, and sequence logic; timing window; confidence score; calibration and referee override logic. | Separate a potentially patentable technical method from sport rules themselves. Keep detailed thresholds, edge-case rules, and tuning confidential unless they must be disclosed in a patent. |
| **Latency-aware referee evidence pipeline** | Synchronisation of motion, video, timing, event logic, interface alerts, and tamper-evident replay records; fallback behaviours when a sensor or link fails. | Patent screen where the architecture resolves a real-time technical problem. Keep security keys, diagnostic heuristics, and operational tuning as secrets. |
| **Decision-confidence and human-in-the-loop interface** | How confidence, conflicting signals, replay evidence, and decision recommendation are calculated and presented so an official can act safely within a time bound. | Potential technical-interface claim only if it produces a technical effect, not just a presentation of information. The visual interface itself is copyright/design material. |
| **Portable venue deployment architecture** | Minimum hardware topology, timing/communication layout, automatic venue mapping, and the way it reduces set-up time or restores calibration after disturbance. | May be patentable if technically novel; otherwise protect as confidential deployment know-how and a packaged service. |

## 2. Assets that should remain confidential

These are likely more valuable as trade secrets than as public patent disclosures, provided VLI can actually control access. A trade secret is not a right against independent development, so it needs operational discipline.

| Confidential asset | Why it matters | Minimum control |
|---|---|---|
| Event-classification thresholds, confidence weights, and edge-case handling | A competitor may reproduce the equipment but struggle to match stable decision quality. | Role-based access, private repositories, encrypted configuration, version control, NDA/IP clauses, and an internal access log. |
| Calibration and deployment playbook | This can reduce a multi-hour expert process into repeatable event-day operation. | Split customer-facing checklists from internal set-up logic; deliver sensitive operating steps only to trained staff or licensed operators. |
| Labelled match data, replay library, failure cases, and referee annotations | The dataset can become the most defensible asset and may support future models. | Data-rights agreements, athlete/event consent, controlled storage, provenance records, retention schedule, and no raw-data access by default. |
| Supplier pricing, hardware bill of materials, installation cost, and venue feasibility database | These enable margin and deployment advantage. | Limit to finance/operations users; bind contractors and suppliers to confidentiality. |
| Pilot feedback, unresolved risk register, and commercial price tests | These show which features buyers value and prevent a copycat from learning VLI’s route to market. | Store in restricted systems; do not place in public proposals or general marketing decks. |

## 3. Copyright, design, and database assets

| Asset | Likely protection route | Practical action |
|---|---|---|
| Source code, APIs, rule-authoring tools, Unity project files, test scripts, and deployment software | Copyright; confidentiality while not public. | Company ownership agreement from every founder, employee, contractor, and contributor; retain repository and commit records; maintain an open-source software bill of materials. |
| Decision dashboard, referee interface, replay visualisation, icons, technical manuals, videos, website copy, and diagrams | Copyright; potentially registered design for distinctive physical/visual product appearance. | Put the company copyright notice in software and documents; use versioned design files; ensure all third-party assets are licensed. |
| Structured annotated datasets and event taxonomies | Copyright/database-related rights may apply depending on original selection/arrangement; contractual data rights are still essential. | Use event-host and participant agreements to secure rights to collect, use, improve, and create derived data products. |
| Hardware enclosure, camera mount, portable case, goal-ring sensor housing, and branded installation layout | Registered design where visual appearance is commercially valuable; trade secret for functional details. | Photograph and preserve CAD/design history; do not launch publicly before deciding whether to file a design application. |

## 4. Brand, reputation, and standards assets

| Asset | Protection route | Practical action |
|---|---|---|
| **Velocity Lab Innovation**, **VLI**, product names, logos, and taglines | Trade-mark registrations in the relevant jurisdictions and service classes. | Search for clearance first; file company/product marks before major international promotion; own related domains and social handles. |
| “VLI Certified Referee System” or equivalent conformity mark | Registered trade mark plus a controlled certification/licensing programme. | Do not call it “FAI certified”, “FAI approved”, or “official” unless a written FAI authorisation specifically permits it. |
| FAI/CIAM relationship, pilot-host references, and competition credibility | Contract and endorsement permissions, not IP owned by VLI. | Obtain logo/name permissions and define relationship labels exactly: pilot host, collaborator, sponsor, purchaser, or standards participant. |
| Referee accreditation/training course and operator handbook | Copyright, contract, and trade mark. | Make VLI training, system-installation certification, annual audit, and software updates part of the commercial model. |

## 5. Ownership and evidence gaps to close immediately

1. Confirm that a single company—not the individual founders—owns all code, CAD, data, documents, domains, product names, and future patent rights. Execute invention-assignment and confidentiality clauses before further development.
2. Produce a dated invention disclosure for every patent candidate, signed by contributors, with diagrams, technical problem, alternatives, results, and test evidence.
3. Freeze an internal list of every public disclosure already made, including website copy, demo video, HKSTP material, competition demonstrations, and partner presentations. Patent counsel must assess whether any public statement affects novelty.
4. Separate third-party components from VLI assets: OptiTrack, Unity, open-source libraries, venue data, FAI rules, and partner branding must never be represented as VLI-owned IP.
5. Conduct a patentability search and a separate freedom-to-operate search before committing to a technical architecture. The first asks whether VLI can obtain a patent; the second asks whether VLI may commercialise without infringing someone else’s rights.
