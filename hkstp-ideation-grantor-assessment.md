# HKSTP Ideation Grantor-Style Assessment: Velocity Lab Innovation

**Prepared by:** Manus AI  
**Review basis:** The public Velocity Lab Innovation website and linked Product, Use Cases, Pricing, and People pages, reviewed on 13 August 2026. This is a simulated HKSTP Ideation panel assessment, not an official HKSTP decision.

## Executive assessment

> **Indicative panel view: promising domain-led concept, but not yet grant-ready on the public evidence available.**

Velocity Lab Innovation presents a coherent early concept: a drone-sports decision-support system combining OptiTrack motion capture, configurable rules, replay, and a Unity-based decision interface. The narrow initial context of drone sports is more credible than a generic “AI for sport” pitch, and the founder profiles show relevant UAV, engineering, robotics, and competition exposure. The public site is also polished and communicates a clear aspiration: more defensible and evidence-backed calls.

However, I would **not recommend funding solely from the current website and pitch framing**. The proposal reads primarily as a configurable integration of established components rather than a clearly differentiated technology venture. It provides no independently verifiable performance data, signed pilot evidence, stated buyer budget, unit economics, product IP, or measurable development milestones. HKSTP positions Ideation for a ground-breaking idea supported by both **R&D and business planning**, and the grant is milestone assessed; these are precisely the areas where the public case remains thin.[1][2]

The right conclusion is **“proceed to panel only with an evidence pack and a materially sharper R&D-to-market story.”** Do not solve the gaps with stronger adjectives. Solve them with traceable prototype results, scoped customer commitments, a defined proprietary workstream, and a funding plan whose milestones are objectively assessable.

| Dimension | Current impression | Grantor conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Problem relevance | Credible but not quantified. Fast drone-sports calls and replay disputes are plausible pain points. | **Promising**, but customer pain and economic cost must be demonstrated. |
| Technical proposition | Sensor, rules, replay, and interface form a coherent system. | **Unclear novelty.** The applicant must separate VLI IP from OptiTrack, Unity, and standard integration work. |
| Market and revenue | Rental and one-off purchase are named; classes and prices are opaque. | **Immature.** There is no buyer, pricing, sales cycle, margin, or market-size evidence. |
| Team | Founders appear technically and domain relevant. | **Good early signal**, but commercial, sports-governance, and deployment capacity are unaddressed. |
| Traction | Partner logos and an embedded demo are visible. | **Insufficiently evidenced.** Relationship type, pilot status, outcomes, and commercial commitment are not stated. |
| Fundability | A reasonable pre-incubation ambition. | **Conditional.** Fund only against tightly designed technical and commercial milestones. |

## What a real Ideation panel is likely to test

HKSTP describes Ideation as a one-year programme for applicants with a ground-breaking idea proven through R&D and business planning. It offers up to HKD 100,000, with funding disbursed in three tranches subject to satisfactory assessment of three milestones; shortlisted applicants undergo a panel assessment and online due diligence.[1][2][3] The public material does not disclose a formal scoring weighting, so the following questions are a grantor inference from those published requirements rather than claimed official scoring criteria.

The key question is therefore not whether motion capture can record motion. It is whether **Velocity Lab can develop and commercialise a defensible innovation** that a sports organiser will pay for, and whether HKSTP funding will turn the current concept into objectively stronger technical, customer, and venture evidence within one year.

## The most defensible panel answers

These are **answer structures**, not claims to repeat verbatim. Use them only after collecting the stated proof. An unsupported confident answer will fail due diligence more quickly than a candid early-stage answer.

| Panel question | A disciplined answer structure |
|---|---|
| “Why is this innovative?” | “OptiTrack is our sensor foundation, not the innovation claim. Our R&D is the competition-specific layer that converts calibrated trajectories into auditable events under real match constraints. We can show the architecture, the owner of each IP component, and results against a baseline manual/video review process.” |
| “Why now, and why drone sport?” | “Drone sport is a focused beachhead, not a vague market. It gives us direct access to real event organisers and defined spatial rules. We have evidence from [number] discovery interviews and [number] scoped pilot partners that [specific decision failure] causes [specific delay, dispute, or operating cost].” |
| “Who pays?” | “The initial buyer is [named organiser/venue/association role], not ‘the drone community’. They buy [event rental / annual venue service] because it addresses [named operational or fairness outcome]. We have tested a price range and are measuring service cost before setting a repeatable package.” |
| “Will it replace referees?” | “No. Our first product is human-in-the-loop decision support. It creates a consistent and reviewable reference; officials retain authority. This reduces adoption, governance, and liability risk while giving us a realistic validation path.” |
| “What have you proved?” | “We distinguish completed work from planned work. Completed: [prototype / controlled test / pilot]. Measured result: [metric, test conditions, sample size]. Not yet proved: [risk]. This grant specifically funds the experiment that resolves that risk.” |
| “Why should HKSTP fund this rather than a customer?” | “The requested HKD 100,000 pays for early R&D and pilot validation that a customer cannot reasonably fund before technical feasibility is demonstrated. Each tranche converts uncertainty into an asset: validated event detection, a refereed pilot outcome, and a repeatable commercial deployment plan.” |
| “What happens if OptiTrack is costly or venue installation is difficult?” | “We have documented the hardware, calibration, transport, and operator cost per event. We are testing a rental-first model and a minimum viable set-up. We will not claim broad deployment until the observed deployment time and unit economics meet the threshold in our milestone plan.” |

## Suggested three-milestone grant plan

The following design fits HKSTP’s published milestone-based funding structure, but the founders must replace bracketed placeholders with real commitments and defensible targets. Avoid claiming a metric before the test protocol has been agreed.[1]

| Stage | Objective | Acceptable evidence | Commercial learning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Milestone 1 — define and de-risk** | Specify the initial sport, rule events, calibration workflow, and novelty hypothesis; complete an alpha decision-support prototype. | Requirements signed off by a pilot host; architecture and IP map; controlled-test report measuring the agreed tracking, event-classification, and latency metrics; data and safety protocol. | Confirm buyer role, current workflow, problem severity, deployment constraints, and a preliminary paid-pilot price range. |
| **Milestone 2 — run a governed pilot** | Run an instrumented pilot in a representative arena with a human referee retained as final authority. | Pilot agreement; test log; referee comparison; failure-mode analysis; participant/venue permissions; a documented iteration from pilot feedback. | Measure willingness to pay, event-day set-up cost, staffing needs, sales cycle, and conversion conditions. |
| **Milestone 3 — prove repeatability** | Prepare a repeatable package for the beachhead segment and secure next-step customer validation. | Deployment playbook; customer acceptance criteria; product roadmap; IP and data-governance plan; results of a repeat or paid pilot. | A defined configuration, transparent quote logic, estimated contribution margin, and at least one evidence-backed next commercial step. |

## Website and pitch changes before submission

The current visual standard is strong, but several elements reduce a grant panel’s confidence. The Home page visibly labels three graphics as **“Ecosystem image placeholder,” “Workflow image placeholder,” and “Roadmap image placeholder.”** Its supporting metrics are also labels—“Live,” “Real-time,” “Data,” and “Multi”—rather than measured results. A reviewer will interpret this as an unfinished evidence story, not simply unfinished design.

| Current phrasing or element | Grantor concern | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| “Precision-driven… powered by OptiTrack motion capture” and “AI-powered officiating” | VLI’s own technology is not visible; AI claim may be ambiguous. | One plain diagram that identifies sensor layer, VLI-built layer, data flow, human referee role, and current versus planned capabilities. |
| “Millimeter-level”, “instant”, and “real-time” | These are performance claims without system-level validation. | A short test-method block: metric, environment, sample size, result, date, and definition of the decision being measured. Remove the claim until proof exists. |
| “and beyond” / “Multi sport ready” | Looks like premature scope expansion. | “Initial focus: [specific drone-sport format]. Adjacent applications under validation: [only those with a named pilot or study].” |
| Partner logos | Could imply endorsement, partnership, or revenue where none exists. | Caption every logo with the relationship type and written permission; show an anonymised or named pilot only with consent. |
| “Class 20” / “Class 40” | Buyer cannot infer space, hardware, service, or price implications. | A configuration sheet defining capacity, venue assumptions, equipment, set-up time, operator support, and quote drivers. |
| Generic roadmap | Does not support grant milestone assessment. | A dated one-year roadmap with R&D hypothesis, pilot, decision criteria, funding use, and measurable outputs. |
| Founder biographies only | Technical credibility is present but commercial execution is unaddressed. | Add a compact operating-team section naming commercial, event-operations, and sport-governance coverage, including advisers where applicable. |

### Tighter opening narrative

The website can retain its strong creative line, but the funding version needs a clearer investment thesis. A credible version would be:

> “Velocity Lab is developing a human-in-the-loop decision-support system for **[specific drone-sport format]**. It uses calibrated motion capture to turn defined match events—such as **[two verified events]**—into auditable referee evidence. Our R&D focuses on **[VLI-owned technical layer]** under live competition constraints. We will validate the system through **[named/scoped pilot]**, then commercialise a **[rental / venue-service]** offer for **[defined buyer].”

This narrative is much stronger because it names the sport, technical work, buyer, validation route, and revenue path. It should be adapted to facts the team can document; it should not become a new unsupported claim.

## Decision recommendation

I would give the team a **“revise and re-present”** rather than an immediate decline. The founder-product fit and initial problem choice are credible enough to justify a short path to a fuller panel submission. Yet I would insist on four preconditions before recommending Ideation support:

1. **A proprietary R&D thesis:** identify the VLI-owned innovation and the technical risk to be resolved; do not present an integration stack as the innovation.
2. **Customer proof:** obtain at least two specific, scoped customer or pilot commitments, ideally with one paid or with a clearly documented willingness-to-pay test.
3. **Measured evidence:** report system-level accuracy, availability, latency, and operational set-up results under stated conditions; keep the human referee in the validation loop.
4. **A milestone and commercial plan:** show exactly how the grant changes the venture over one year, including use of funds, deployment costs, governance, and a repeatable revenue model.

If the founders cannot produce this evidence, the more honest positioning is a **technical services/pilot project**, not an HKSTP Ideation startup yet. If they can produce it, the project has a credible route to being a focused sports-tech and spatial-decision venture with broader applications tested later—not assumed today.
