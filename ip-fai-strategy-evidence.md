# IP and FAI Strategy Evidence

## FAI governance and drone-sport context

FAI places drone sport in the CIAM F9 category. CIAM establishes and maintains rules, criteria, safety requirements, and technical standards for aeromodelling competitions. Each FAI Member Country may appoint a delegate who participates in CIAM work and has a voting role at the Plenary Meeting. The CIAM public proposal page shows formal Sporting Code proposal forms and a submission process.

The current F9A Drone Soccer rules explicitly preserve human referee authority: the scoring referee decides whether a goal is recognised, while an electronic sensor may be installed on the goal ring if it does not interfere with the scorer drone ball. This supports positioning VLI as evidence-led decision support or an approved technical standard, rather than claiming that a proprietary product can unilaterally become the official referee.

The Aero Club of Hong Kong is the FAI Active Member for Hong Kong, China, acts as the Hong Kong National Air Sport Control organisation, and appoints FAI Commission delegates. Its published list identifies a CIAM/Aeromodelling delegate. A FAI technical or rule proposal should therefore be developed with, and sponsored through, the relevant Hong Kong NAC and CIAM channel rather than sent directly to FAI as a vendor request.

## Hong Kong IP context

Hong Kong IPD states that patents are territorial and needed to stop unauthorised manufacture, use, sale, or import in Hong Kong. Software is generally protected by copyright, while a computer-related invention can be patentable where it solves a technical problem and delivers a further technical effect. IPD also states that invention details must remain confidential before filing, because pre-filing disclosure may destroy novelty.
